Lesson 4 · 20 min

Eliminate, simplify, redesign

With the map in hand, the temptation is to pick the slowest step and put an agent on it. Before that come three verbs, in this order: eliminate what doesn't need to exist, simplify what's needed but too big, and redesign what's in the wrong place. In this lesson you take apart Horizonte's process and talk with Carlos, who wanted to skip all of this.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1The three verbs of redesign, in the right order
  2. 2Find who depended on the step you cut
  3. 3Replace "check everything" with exception and sampling
  4. 4Move the check to where the error starts

Objective: take the process apart before automating: cut, simplify, and move steps, with the reason for each change written down.

01 · Three verbs before automating

Each step uses the map from lesson 3. Automating comes last. Click each step.

What always stays: real requirement, judgment, and exception. They change place and size, but they don't disappear.

Competency 2 of the AI Management Area (INEMA): should this process still exist? Eliminate, simplify, redesign, and only then automate.

02 · Horizonte's redesign

From 13 steps (v1.0) to 8 (v2.0). Fictional study company; the discount policy is the same as in the agents course.

1. Eliminate

Start with the H and P steps on the tagging sheet. For each one, ask: who used what this step produced? Every step you cut fed someone, even if it was just a Friday report.

Cut only when that person has another source. Otherwise the habit comes back in hiding: someone recreates the spreadsheet on their own the following week.

At HorizonteThe sales spreadsheet (step 3) fed Patrícia's goals report; the report now comes out of the app. The paper (6), the notice (9) and the email (10) disappear once inventory and finance see the order on screen.

2. Simplify

Checking everything is born from a scare and stays forever. Within two weeks, someone who approves a hundred things a day approves without reading, and the check turns into a rubber stamp.

Swap it for two things: the exceptions the rule already defines, checked one by one, and a small sample of the common cases, looked at every week to see if the rule is still right.

At HorizontePatrícia stops checking 170 orders a week. She sees only the discounts between 5% and 10% and the orders above R$ 5 thousand (fictional study policy), plus 10 common orders drawn at random every Monday.

3. Redesign

The costliest error is the one found late. If a rule can be checked as soon as the case arrives, checking it at the end only guarantees rework: the case has already moved on, and it comes back.

Move the check to where the error is born, and merge the steps that were only separate because they belonged to different people.

At HorizonteAn order only enters the app with a customer whose record is complete. A new customer goes first to Juliana to be registered. Case P3, which came back from finance and took 75 hours, stops existing this way.

4. Only then automate

The redesigned version has fewer steps, and each one has a reason. Now it makes sense to ask who does each one, and the answer comes in the next lesson.

Some steps don't change owners: the approval above the policy amount, the invoice, and the judgment calls. They get smaller and better informed, not automatic.

At Horizontev2.0: order on WhatsApp → built in the app only with a complete customer record → policy applied, exceptions go to Patrícia → inventory sees it on screen → picks and records the stock removal → order goes to the ERP without retyping → invoice → invoice to the customer.

    v1 = 13 steps · v2 = 8 steps: 5 eliminated, 4 and 5 merged into a single queue, and an automatic handoff from the app to the ERP in place of retyping.

    1. The customer orders via WhatsApp; the Hora or Juliana replies.
    2. Order built in the app only with a complete record; a new customer goes to Juliana first to be registered.
    3. The policy becomes a fixed rule in the app: discounts up to 5% and orders up to R$ 5 thousand go through; discounts from 5% to 10% and orders above R$ 5 thousand go to Patrícia, one by one; above 10%, only Carlos. Every Monday, 10 ordinary orders drawn at random.
    4. The warehouse sees the order on screen and checks availability.
    5. It picks and writes off stock in the app.
    6. The order goes from the app to the ERP without retyping.
    7. Finance checks and issues the invoice.
    8. The invoice goes to the customer through the conversation, with a record.

    Who does each step is still provisional: that's lesson 5. Writing the agent's authority limit action by action is course G3.

    • A cut with no new source. If the "who used it" column is empty, someone will quietly recreate the step.
    • "Manual" as the reason. Manual is not a defect; habit and handoff are.
    • Requirement cut. Invoice, approval of an amount, and legal obligations stay, even if their form changes.
    • A check the person who entered it approves herself. Whoever enters does not approve.
    • Automating on the sheet. The "who does it" column is provisional; don't write "agent" before lesson 5.

    2 · Horizonte's version 2.0

    25 min

    Use the map you made in lesson 3 and the tagging sheet from lesson 1. Fill in the redesign sheet for the 13 steps without looking at the tab, and only then compare. Run the prompt about what could break.

    3 · The redesigned version of your process

    45 min

    Build the redesign sheet for your process from the map in lesson 3. Show the new version to the people who carry out the eliminated steps and ask what they would lose. Adjust the sheet with their answers.

    Done when: the sheet has every step from v1 with a verb and a reason, at least 2 steps cut or moved with a reason and a new source, the v1 × v2 count, the list of what stays with people, and the name of who you consulted.

    07 · Summary

    EliminateHabit and handoff go, after finding who depended on them.
    SimplifyChecking everything becomes exceptions one by one plus a weekly sample.
    RedesignVerification where the error starts; steps joined in a single system.
    Only then automateThe agent gets the redesigned process, never "do it the same way."
