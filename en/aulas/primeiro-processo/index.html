Lesson 2 · 15 min

Choosing the first process

Carlos wants the agent in billing; a salesperson already pastes the customer spreadsheet into a personal chatbot; the support summary runs on its own at 6 p.m. Each one started from a different side, and nobody chose. In this lesson you compare the candidates by value, feasibility, and risk, pick one, write down why not the others, and define the scope.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Three questions that take off the list what isn't worth the effort
  2. 2Score value, feasibility, and risk without guesswork
  3. 3Why one process at a time
  4. 4Write down the choice, the scope, and what is left for later

Objective: prioritize processes by value, feasibility, and risk in a matrix and justify in writing the one you chose and what stays out.

02 · How to choose

Four steps. Horizonte's scores (fictional study company) are in the model alongside.

1. List and filter

Start with the list and run the three questions. A process that happens once a month, with just one person, rarely pays off the work of mapping, measuring, and redesigning.

Put on the list what has already been automated without anyone deciding. An AI tool that someone turned on by their own initiative is a process redesigned on the sly, with a risk nobody has measured.

At HorizonteFive candidates: order by WhatsApp through to the invoice; collection of overdue payment slips (what Carlos wants); daily service summary; proposal for a large client (the salesperson uses a personal chatbot); replenishment purchasing from suppliers.

2. Three scores with a reason

Value: volume per week, people's time, rework, customers waiting. Feasibility: the process has an owner, the rules are written down, the data is in a system you can read. Risk: money, personal data, customers, things that can't be undone.

Every score gets a one-sentence reason. Without that sentence, the score is just the preference of whoever filled it in, and the matrix only dresses guesswork in new clothes.

Collections, at HorizonteValue 4 (money sitting idle). Feasibility 2: there is no baseline, and the negotiation rules are in Carlos's head. Risk 5: it involves money and the customer. Total 7: it waits, with a reason.

3. One at a time

The KPMG Global Tech Report 2026 (survey, 04/2026) reports that 45% of Brazilian executives have several AI projects disconnected from one another. Each area starts on its own, and nobody can say what worked.

One process at a time, start to finish, with measurement. The second one starts when the first has a baseline, a version, and an owner, not when the excitement kicks in.

At HorizonteThe sales agent, the service summary, the email assistant, and the team's personal chatbots are already four initiatives that nobody has brought together. IT's inventory doesn't know about half of them.

4. Choose and scope

Write three sentences: why this process, why not the runner-up in the matrix, and why not the one the boss asked for (if it's a different one). Then the pilot scope: which cases go into the pilot and which stay out at the start.

Scoping is not an excuse to pick the easy one. It's what lets you measure the before and after without mixing cases that behave in different ways.

At HorizonteChosen: order by WhatsApp through to the invoice (total 12). Orders from registered customers are in. Out at the start: new customers, exchanges and returns, sales made by a representative.

Prompt · devil's advocate for the choice
You are an experienced, skeptical manager. Below is the matrix I used to
choose the first process to redesign with AI in my area.
Don't choose for me. Play the role of someone who disagrees:

1. Point out scores whose reason doesn't support the number given.
2. Tell me which risk I may have underestimated in each process.
3. Give the best argument in favor of the runner-up.
4. Tell me whether the scope leaves out something that should be in.

Simulation: illustrative example

Sales and operations · Patrícia · Horizonte, fictional study company · fictional scores

ProcessVBRTotal
Order by WhatsApp through to the invoice54312
Replenishment purchasing from suppliers33210
Daily service summary2448
Proposal for a large client3348
Collection of overdue payment slips4257
  • Order → invoice: about 170 orders per week, rework in invoicing (V 5); the app and ERP exist and the discount policy is written down (B 4); it touches pricing, but the invoice still goes through people (R 3).
  • Daily summary: sends conversations with personal data to a service outside the country with no contract (R 4). Before improving it, it needs fixing.
  • Proposal: today it uses the customer spreadsheet in a personal chatbot, outside the inventory (R 4).

Chosen: order → invoice. Not replenishment: less volume and fewer customers waiting. Not collections: no baseline and the highest risk; it moves up when invoicing is measured.

  • Score without a reason. Reread each number and check whether the sentence justifies it. If you would change the 4 to a 3 without changing the sentence, the score is a guess.
  • Risk added as an advantage. High risk has to lower the total (6 − R).
  • Process with no ends. "Sales" is not a process; "order by WhatsApp through to the invoice" is.
  • Picking the showiest one. The first process is the one you can measure and finish, not the one that impresses in the meeting.
  • Empty queue. If the other four have no reason or condition to move up, someone will start them on their own.

04 · Practice

The Horizonte matrix to calibrate your scores; yours for real.

1 · The Horizonte matrix

20 min

Open the IT inventory and the service summary piece in the lab. Redo the matrix for the five processes with your own scores, without copying the ones on the tab. Where your score differs by 2 points or more, write down your argument. Run the devil's advocate prompt.

Done when: all 5 processes have the three scores, each score with a one-sentence reason, the total calculated, the chosen one with the three sentences of justification (including why not collections), and the scope with at least 2 exclusions.

2 · The matrix for your area

40 min

List the processes in your area that pass the filter, including the ones someone has already automated without agreeing on it. Pick 5 and fill in the matrix. Show it to someone who works on them and adjust the scores they challenge with a good reason.

Done when: the matrix has 5 processes with a start and an end in the name, 15 scores with reasons, the chosen one justified in 3 sentences, the pilot scope, and the queue with the reason each one is not now.

Lab pieces: IT inventory and service summary (fictional study company).
