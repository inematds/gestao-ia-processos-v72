Lesson 6 · 20 min

Baseline and target

Every Monday Carlos opens the agent's dashboard and says: "look how it's climbing!" It really is climbing. But the dashboard started counting the day the agent came on board, and it only shows volume. Without the before, any rise looks like a win, and any redesign has no proof. In this lesson you pick three indicators with a denominator and record today's value before you change anything.

Start the lesson

In this lesson you will

  1. 1Why volume goes up without proving anything
  2. 2Write an indicator as a fraction, with a period and a source
  3. 3Mark what is observed and what is a hypothesis
  4. 4A target with a deadline and the guardrail for what can't get worse

Objective: define 3 indicators with a numerator, a denominator, and today's value, observed or marked as a hypothesis, before changing the process.

01 · An indicator in five parts

From the question to the guardrail. Tap each part.

Click each block of the diagram to see what it does.

Indicator rules from the Gestoria method (INEMA): always period, population, denominator, and source; test, pilot, and production kept separate.

02 · From the dashboard that climbs to the baseline

The dashboard numbers come from the lab (fictional study company). Open the piece and check.

1. The dashboard that climbs

Hora's dashboard shows conversations rising from 702 (week of 09/01) to 901 (week of 10/13), and closed orders from 141 to 168. The collection started the same day the agent came on board. There is no before and no comparison.

Satisfaction comes from a star-rating button that about 6% of customers answer. An average of those who answered doesn't speak for the other 94%.

The math the dashboard doesn't doOrders ÷ conversations: 141 ÷ 702 = 20.1% on 09/01; 168 ÷ 901 = 18.6% on 10/13. Volume went up and conversion went down. "Climbing" doesn't prove the agent helped.

2. Three process indicators

For the order-to-invoice process, three questions are enough: how long it takes, how much comes back, how much people's work it uses. Each one becomes a fraction with a period and a source.

Time is measured by the median, not the average: one order stuck for three days pulls the average up and hides the normal. Add the typical worst case (the p95) so you don't lose sight of the stuck ones.

At HorizonteTime from confirmed order to invoice (median and p95 of the orders invoiced in the week). Orders returned ÷ orders that reached billing. People's minutes ÷ orders invoiced.

3. Observed or hypothesis

The five cases in lesson 3 give you a clue, not the baseline. The baseline comes from four weeks counted in the systems, before any change. Until then, today's value goes in marked as a hypothesis, with the date it becomes observed.

Freed-up people time is not saved money either: it only becomes savings if the company cuts an expense or uses that time on something you can verify.

At HorizonteToday: 30 h until the invoice and 21 min of people time (median of the 5 cases, hypothesis); about 9% sent back ("about 15 a week" ÷ 168, stated, hypothesis). Count in the app and in the ERP for four weeks before version 2.0.

4. Target and guardrail

A goal is a value and a deadline, decided by whoever answers for the process, looking at the baseline and not at wishful thinking. Revise the goal when the observed baseline arrives: it may show the hypothesis was far off.

The guardrail protects what cannot get worse. If the goal is speed, the guardrail is error: a discount outside the policy, a wrong invoice, a complaint.

At Horizonte (fictional decision)Goals for 8 weeks after version 2.0: median until the invoice of 24 h or less; returns of 3% or less; people minutes per order under 15. Guardrail: zero orders with a discount outside the policy.

  • A count without a denominator. "15 returned" in a week of 100 orders is very different from in a week of 300.
  • Average time. One stuck case pulls the average up. Use the median and p95.
  • A hypothesis without a date. Every hypothesis says when it becomes observed.
  • Different periods. Comparing four year-end weeks with four weeks in February does not measure the redesign.
  • Freed-up time sold as savings. It only becomes savings with a reduced expense or a verified use.

04 · Practice

Fix Horizonte's dashboard; then measure your own process before changing it.

06 · Summary

Volume misleadsWithout the before and without a denominator, "goes up" proves nothing.
Full fractionNumerator, denominator, period, and source.
Today, markedObserved over four counted weeks, or a dated hypothesis.
Target and guardrailValue and deadline, without worsening what cannot get worse.
